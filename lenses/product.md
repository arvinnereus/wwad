# Lens: Product (scope, features, roadmap)

For feature decisions, scope calls, roadmaps, and "should this exist" questions. These are the tests
unique to the product altitude — the doctrine's "say no" and "it just works" already apply; this is how
to wield them on scope.

## Does this need to exist?
The most Apple question there is. For the feature in front of you, would Apple ship it — or refuse it to
protect the focus of the whole? Most features are added to silence a vocal user, match a competitor's
checklist, or dodge a hard "no." Name which of those is really driving it, then decide.

## Feature-creep audit: removal is a feature
Separate the features that serve the core job from the ones that accreted around it like barnacles on a
hull — slowly, invisibly, until the product is heavy. List what could be cut and what the product would
*gain* in clarity and speed by cutting it. Note where a settings panel is hiding decisions the team
should have made (that's doctrine #4 — convert toggles to defaults).

## Optimize the 90% case
Is the product shaped around the common case, or bloated to cover edge cases that tax everyone? Apple
will cheerfully decline to serve the 10% when serving them degrades the experience for the 90%. Find
the edge-case machinery making the main path heavier and question whether it earns its weight.

## The smallest lovable version
What is the smallest version someone would genuinely *love*, not merely tolerate? Apple ships less, but
finished. Identify the lovable core and the things that can wait for a later release without anyone
feeling the product is incomplete today.

## Finish fewer things
Half-built features are worse than absent ones — they make the whole product feel like a beta and erode
trust. Better three things that feel complete than seven that feel provisional. Flag anything present
but unpolished and force the choice: finish it or cut it.
