# Lens: Internal (tools, workflows, automations, team process)

For internal tools, recurring workflows, automations, and the way a team or business runs. The core
insight: **an internal experience deserves the same product rigor as a customer-facing one.** These
are the internal-altitude tests; the doctrine's integration (#3) and privacy (#6) principles apply
inward too — reference them rather than restating them.

## Dogfood standard
Would you ship this internal experience to a paying customer? If not, why tolerate it yourself? Teams
accept friction internally they'd never inflict on a user. Hold the internal tool to the customer bar
and the gaps become obvious — that's the whole lens in one question.

## Default to automated
What manual step recurs often enough that it should simply be invisible? "It just works" applies
inward: the best internal step is the one a human never performs. Find the repeated manual action and
ask what it would take to make it disappear.

## One clear owner
Does this system, tool, or process have a single obvious owner — or is it everyone's-and-therefore-
no-one's? Shared-by-default ownership is where quality quietly rots. Name the owner, or name the
problem.

## Subtract the process
Which steps, meetings, approvals, or fields exist out of habit rather than need? Process accretes like
features do. For each one, ask what actually breaks if it's gone. Most habitual process survives only
because no one has said that question out loud.

## Where seams and data pile up
Internal workflows are where tool-to-tool handoffs leak time (doctrine #3 — count the copy-pastes and
tab-switches between systems and collapse them) and where secrets, credentials, and personal data
quietly accumulate (doctrine #6 — who can access what, and what's stored "just in case"). The
integration and privacy bets don't stop at the customer boundary; apply them to the back office too.
